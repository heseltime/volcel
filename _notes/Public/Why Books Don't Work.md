Andy Matuschak's essay [[Why books don't work::https://andymatuschak.org/books/]] asks why explanatory non-fiction so often fails to leave readers understanding what they read. In his words: "In these brief notes, we'll explore why books so often don't work, and why they succeed when they do."

His diagnosis, as I read it: books and lectures both lean on an implicit model where knowledge transfers by absorbing the author's words, and that model is mostly wrong.[^transmission] Understanding comes from what the reader does with the ideas. The essay goes on to ask what a medium designed around how people actually learn would look like.

This site is a small, practical answer to that question for my own notes. [[Stacked Notes]] keeps the context of a thought on screen while you follow it, instead of making the reader rebuild it after every click. The notes themselves stay short and linked, closer to working notes than to chapters.
