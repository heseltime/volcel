What I care about most these days wouldn't do well on Instagram or LinkedIn. It is quieter and more local: church music, a choir, a theatre group, Altmünster on the Traunsee, where I grew up, and a slow question about what a life is for.

By day I'm a software engineer at Dynatrace, working in observability: making large, busy systems legible to the people who run them. I got there by a winding road, from literature and film in New York to software engineering and AI in Linz.[^road]

## Why "volcel"

Short for *voluntarily celibate*, meant lightly. It points at a journey toward a religious community and a life that takes prayer and work equally seriously. I don't know yet where it ends; this is one place where I walk it out loud.

Altmünster is named for an abbey recorded there in 909, and its church is dedicated to St. Benedict.[^minster] Along the shore in Traunkirchen, the Jesuits held the old convent from 1622 until 1773. I grew up between those two churches; the small sun on this site nods to the Jesuits' emblem.

## A bus ride

In C.S. Lewis's *The Great Divorce*, a bus lifts off from a grey town where it always rains and carries its passengers to foothills so real the grass hurts their feet.[^lewis] What stays with me is the ride itself: having left one place and not yet arrived at the other.

That is what these pages are for: a place between work, creative time and spiritual life, where writing declutters how I think about important work, so there is more room for creativity and inner space.

My day job and this inner work ask similar questions. What is signal and what is noise? Where does attention actually go? And could the habits of observability (instrument, look, simplify, look again) help declutter a life, or would that just add another dashboard? That thread starts at [[Observability for Inner Space]].
