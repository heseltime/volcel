Working notes, not conclusions. The starting question: what would it mean to observe an inner life the way we observe a running system, and would that help or just add noise?

In software, observability means a system can tell you what it is doing from the signals it already gives off, without you having to guess. The opposite isn't silence; it's a flood of logs no one reads. A life can be like that too: busy, loud and still opaque to the person living it.[^legible]

The examen offers one shape for this. At the end of the day you look back, notice where you were present and where you weren't, and let that shape tomorrow. It is a review, not a verdict. That is close to what a good incident review tries to be when it works: blameless, curious, aimed at seeing.

Open questions I want to keep coming back to:

- Which signals are worth keeping, and which are only noise that feels like information?
- Can a tool help with simplifying, or does every tool eventually want more of your attention?
- What is the smallest useful "dashboard" for a day?
